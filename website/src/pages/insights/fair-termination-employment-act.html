Under Kenya’s Employment Act, 2007, a termination is fair only if the employer had a valid reason and followed a fair procedure. Getting either wrong can lead to a claim in the Employment and Labour Relations Court, even where the employee’s conduct or performance was genuinely poor.

A valid reason

The employer must be able to prove the reason for termination, and that the reason is valid and fair. Acceptable reasons relate to the employee’s conduct, capacity or compatibility, or to the employer’s operational requirements. The Act also lists reasons that can never justify dismissal, including pregnancy, going on leave the employee is entitled to, trade union membership or activities, discrimination on grounds such as race, tribe, sex, religion or disability, and bringing a genuine complaint or legal proceedings against the employer.

A fair procedure

Before terminating on grounds of misconduct, poor performance or physical incapacity, the employer must explain the reason to the employee, in a language the employee understands, and allow the employee to respond. The employee is entitled to have a colleague or, where applicable, a shop floor union representative present. In practice that means:

  • a written notice setting out the allegations or concerns;
  • reasonable time to prepare a response;
  • a hearing at which the employee can respond and be accompanied; and
  • a written decision.

Summary dismissal, without notice, is reserved for gross misconduct, and even then the employee must be heard.

Notice and final dues

Unless the contract provides for longer, an employee paid monthly is entitled to at least 28 days’ notice, or pay in lieu. On exit, the employer should pay all wages earned, accrued leave, any other contractual sums, and issue a certificate of service.

Redundancy

Redundancy has its own rules. The employer must give at least one month’s notice of the intended redundancy to the employee or their union, and to the local labour officer; apply fair selection criteria having regard to seniority, skill, ability and reliability; and pay, among other things, severance pay of at least 15 days’ pay for each completed year of service.

The cost of getting it wrong

Where a termination is found unfair, the Court may order reinstatement or re-engagement, or award compensation of up to twelve months’ gross salary, in addition to unpaid dues.
