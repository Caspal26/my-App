Succession is governed in Kenya by the Law of Succession Act. Planning ahead with a valid will, and understanding how an estate is administered, can spare families years of uncertainty and conflict.

Why make a will

A will lets you decide who inherits your property and appoint executors you trust to carry out your wishes. Without one, your estate is distributed according to the rules on intestacy in the Act, which may not reflect your intentions, and the process of appointing administrators can be slower and more contentious.

To be valid, a written will must generally be signed by the person making it and attested by two competent witnesses. Keep it somewhere safe, tell your executors where it is, and review it after major life events such as marriage, the birth of a child or the acquisition of significant property.

The Act also allows dependants, including a spouse and children, to apply to court for reasonable provision if a will does not adequately provide for them, so it is wise to take advice when deciding how to divide your estate.

Grants of representation

Nobody may deal with a deceased person’s property without legal authority from the court. Where there is a will, the executors apply for a grant of probate. Where there is no will, the people entitled apply for letters of administration. The application is published in the Kenya Gazette so that anyone with an objection can come forward.

Taking, selling or otherwise dealing with an estate’s property without a grant is known as intermeddling and is an offence under the Act.

Confirmation of grant

A grant does not by itself allow property to be distributed. The personal representatives must apply for confirmation of the grant, which generally cannot be done until six months after the grant was issued. At confirmation the court approves how the estate will be shared, and the confirmed grant is then used to transfer land, shares, bank accounts and other assets to the beneficiaries.

Practical steps for families

  • Obtain the death certificate and gather documents such as title deeds, share certificates and bank statements.
  • Identify all beneficiaries and dependants early, and keep them informed.
  • Secure property and avoid distributing assets until a grant has been confirmed.
  • Keep proper accounts of the estate’s income and expenses.
