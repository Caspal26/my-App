## Source provenance and limits

The official WordPress project repository (`WordPress/library`, formerly `WordPress/book`) contains *Milestones: The Story of WordPress* and the underlying founder oral-history transcripts. The project states that these files also generate its published book website. The primary interview and the edited historical chapter provide useful cross-checks, but they are **not independent reporting organisations**. Sales figures in Gardner's story are explicitly attributed to his recollection, not presented as audited accounts. The editions avoid disputed superlatives such as calling Revolution the first paid theme.

The Zulip company history reproduces the founder's account of his own leave. The GitHub release verifies the date and actual release, not the private experience independently. The original `blog.zulip.com` post was blocked by the current environment network policy; it was **not** counted as an opened source. The accessed official history is the actual linked source. This is transparently a first-person company account.

The philosophy sources are the GITenberg mirrors of Project Gutenberg e-texts, accessed through `raw.githubusercontent.com`. Each file contains the original e-text header and edition information; the complete passage and section were checked. The Epictetus text identifies Higginson and the later Liberal Arts Press revisions. The Marcus text's endnotes identify the Casaubon translation, whose paragraph numbering differs from modern editions. Do not silently relabel the old translation's V as a modern numbered V.

## Rotation and the remaining editorial work

This is a small reserve with five distinct founders and five distinct incidents. It includes a South African founder, a printing-business partnership, two ordinary changes of approach, a leave episode and a venture that failed to get off the ground. It is **software-heavy**, and does not yet establish the requested long-term industry/geography balance or include a verified Kenyan or healthcare-company founder. Gardner's former laboratory-design work is relevant context, not a healthcare-founder substitute.

The live research scheduler should prioritise well-documented Kenyan and other African founders, healthcare, non-software industries, women founders and businesses that closed or changed course. It must open sources and verify pivotal claims before adding an edition to the reserve. Do not fill a rotation gap by inventing details or labelling a blocked source as verified. General source sites returned explicit network-policy denials during this session; the draft's domain additions do not themselves prove that runtime access changed.

Never republish one of these incidents under a new headline. For example, another article about Gardner's rejected real-estate design is the same event. A genuinely different later episode in his career would need a new event summary, event date and meaningful comparison against the permanent ledger. The initial quote inventory likewise contains these exact lines; avoid recycling their alternate translations as if they were new ideas.

## Additional reserve: a printing business, not software

Added **The two friends who kept the press**, about Benjamin Franklin resolving the debt and partnership of his Philadelphia printing business around 1729–1730. The opened primary source is his *Autobiography*, chapter VII, in the Project Gutenberg edition mirrored by GITenberg. An independent historical cross-check is Holland Thompson's *The Age of Invention* (1921), chapter I, which confirms the equipment funding, partnership buyout and independent business. Franklin's remembered year (about 1729) and Thompson's age-based chronology (twenty-four, approximately 1730) differ; the edition explicitly preserves that uncertainty rather than inventing a precise date. The debt and offer details are attributed to Franklin's first-person account. No claim is made that the later history independently verifies every private conversation.

The fifth exact philosophy excerpt is Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* VIII.1, 1155a, D. P. Chase translation: “no one would choose to live without friends though he should have all the other good things in the world”. This is a clause from a longer sentence; the attribution labels it accordingly. It was checked against the fetched ISO-Latin-1 Project Gutenberg source, `GITenberg/The-Ethics-of-Aristotle_8438/master/8438-8.txt`, with whitespace normalisation only.
